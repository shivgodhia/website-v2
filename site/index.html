The demo is the easy part.

A prompt that looks right on ten examples breaks on the ten-thousandth. Providers retire the model you built on, prices double, and when someone swaps in a new one nobody can say whether it's better or worse. Most teams find out from a customer.

I fix that by building the answer key first. Once you can score a change, every prompt edit and model swap becomes a measured decision instead of a guess.

One pipeline, start to finish

Traba reads about 37,000 resumes a month with an LLM, to work out where each worker has worked and for how long. Here's what fixing it looked like.

  1. Build the answer key

    People reviewed 248 resumes by hand, in an annotation tool I built for it. From then on, "better" had a definition.

    248 reviewed resumes

  2. Find out why it's wrong

    Almost a third of resumes came back with at least one error. Three causes covered most of them. The model didn't know today's date, so "Present" became 2024. It guessed months it couldn't see. And it named the staffing agency instead of the company the person actually worked at.

    31% of resumes had a mistake

  3. Fix the prompt, then re-test

    One fix per cause, each run three times against the answer key. Along the way the comparison caught about ten mistakes in the human labels too.

    31% 8% of resumes had a mistake

  4. Swap the model

    With an answer key, changing models stops being a leap of faith. Claude Haiku matched Gemini's accuracy at about a seventh of the price.

    $0.022 $0.003 per resume

  5. Ship it

    Live in production on 29 October 2026. At current volume that's about $8,300 a year saved, on a pipeline that is now four times less likely to get a resume wrong.

    −86% cost, live since 29 Oct 2026

Grading recruiter calls

Problem An AI grades every screening call to decide whether a worker qualifies for a job. Google retired the model it ran on. The replacement cost 3.6 times more and got 84% of answers right.

What I did Built a 1,793-answer graded set from 167 real calls with the ops team, rewrote the criteria for all 37 questions, merged two model calls into one, then moved it to GPT-6 Luna.

  • 84% 94% accurate
  • 38s 9s median time
  • $871 $38 a month

Testing an AI phone recruiter

Problem Traba screens workers over the phone with nine voice agents. Nobody could tell whether a prompt or model change made the calls better or worse.

What I did A suite of 46 tests, 18 of them replays of real calls mined from 2,400 production calls, scored on 16 metrics. Then a bake-off across six models and a move to the winner.

  • 86% 100% required questions asked
  • 15 4 stalled or silent calls
  • 4× cheaper per minute of LLM time

Trying new models on live traffic, safely

Problem Traba's internal agent can change real data. Testing a new model on it means risking a bad write in production.

What I did A shadow lane. Candidate models answer a sample of live requests next to production with read-only tools, and an LLM judge compares the two answers. Users see no added latency.

  • 731 production runs
  • 414 of 414 attempted writes blocked
  • $0.04 a run

Also: live parcel tracking across four carriers in two days, to close a $240K customer deal. It had failed on 86% of lookups before.
